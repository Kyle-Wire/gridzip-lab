Every experiment from one late-night question: can the location of bits carry information? Each card is a specimen. Its parent is the idea it grew from, and its fate is what the measurement decided.               • Quick experiments, round 1

                round 1 done

                Every feature alone, removed from the champion, in pairs, and stacked best first, on five 1 MB slices (one held out). 349 runs at lpaq level 6, all decode-verified; level 9 agrees within 0.01 points.

                Worth most in the stack: two-layer mixer −1.32%, page structure −1.26%. Topics and immune memory add 0.03% each. The mixer helps every other feature; the context features overlap (grid + regions most). Mixer by topic × structure and learned regimes make it worse.

              • Quick experiments, round 2: the mixer

                round 2 done

                Seven kinds of mixer change on the champion (learning rates, a fast start, extra weight sets, final-layer selector, bias input, an extra APM), each alone on the 1 MB slices, then stacked best first, then rerun on 1, 3 and 10 MB of one slice. 250 runs, all decode-verified.

                The stack saves 0.57% at 1 MB (0.69% held out) but only 0.21% on 10 MB (level 9: 2,081,896 → 2,077,513). Most of the 1 MB gain is faster learning at the start of a file. What holds at 10 MB: a weight set chosen by fine map region (−0.10%) and a bias input (−0.06%). Higher learning rates turn harmful by 10 MB. The quick slices favour fast learners, so learning-rate results need a longer check.

                champion

                in the lpaq host

Level 9 on 10 MB: indirect −0.48%, + sparse −0.57%, + word with bytes −0.68%, all five −0.92% (the word contexts pay off with the bigger hash table).

                • Held: more of what cmix already has

                  held

                • Quick experiments, round 6: adaptive region tree

                  weight set survived

                  From adaptive mesh refinement: one nested tree over the map's cell embedding, splitting the heaviest, most varied leaf first, read at 16, 64 and 256 leaves, in place of (or beside) the two flat k-means clusterings (16 and 128 regions). Costs 1,535 B to send. Contexts at each depth, and a mixer weight set by tree node.

                  108 runs, all decode-verified. As a mixer switch it works: a weight set by 256-leaf node, level 9 on 10 MB, −0.11% (flat k-means control at the same 256-way split: −0.03%), so refining where the text is busy is what helps; with tree contexts −0.17%. Its labels cost 1,280 B (0.06% of 10 MB, under 0.01% of enwik8). As contexts it adds ~0% on 10 MB; on plain lpaq1 the flat regions beat it (−1.01% vs −0.69%); replacing them costs +0.21%. The tree weight set is now in the cmix graft (GZFLAGS 1024) and the queued cmix feature test.

                • Quick experiments, round 7: map-native contexts and map variants

                  next cell survived, small

                  Contexts only our map can give: where the text is inside the trie of map cells (the automaton state), and which cell followed the current cell last time (an indirect context over cells, not bytes). Map variants: 2,048 cells, cells learned from 20 MB instead of 5, regions clustered by what follows a cell, and a two-pass map learned from the test text itself.

                  89 runs, all decode-verified; every number includes what the setting must send. 10 MB: next cell −0.07%, automaton state +0.01%, both −0.05%. On plain lpaq1 each helps (−0.56%, −0.43%), but the region contexts already cover most of it. Maps: 2,048 cells +0.19%, 20 MB −0.01%, follow-only regions +0.05%, two-pass −0.02%; the champion map holds. Level 9 on 10 MB: next cell −0.13%, and it grows with the model (−0.07% at level 6). A small survivor; a candidate for the cmix graft.

                • Round 7b: the correlogram and GIS ideas

                  no gain

                  From an anisotropy correlogram of the text's path across the map (held out, the direction of the last step adds 0.048 bits per token and the recent place 0.035 to the two previous regions), and from GIS: geographically weighted mixing (mixer weights blended between the two nearest regions by inverse distance), watershed regions (quickshift on cell density), a region tree grown at Local Moran's I hotspots (where the bits are spent).

                  96 runs, all decode-verified, map cost counted. 10 MB: direction + recent place +0.08% (+0.02% before its 1,280 B); recent-place weight set +0.07%; blended regions +0.09% (hard regions just as good before cost); watershed regions +0.07% (lopsided: one basin holds 41% of the text, 84 are single cells); hotspot tree −0.025%, the round-6 tree −0.027%. The signals are real but lpaq1g's region contexts already carry them. Surprise analysis: the semivariogram rises smoothly to a plateau only at map distance ~1.35, beyond every region's size, so it can't pick a region count; the 128 k-means regions explain the most of what follows a cell (45%, tree 256: 40%); Moran's I 0.48, hotspots hold 12% of the bits. Level 9 on 10 MB with cost: round-6 tree weight set −0.03%, hotspot tree −0.03%.

                • Article chain walked backwards

                  lost at full scale

                  From the "directed flow" idea: the greedy chain steps from each article to its most similar unused one, so an article's own neighbourhood follows it. Walked backwards, that neighbourhood comes first and primes the model. Same permutation, same cost.

                  10 MB test slice (824 pages), lpaq host level 6: reversed −0.16%, shuffled +1.20% (order is worth ~1.2% at this size), a greedy "best covered next" re-walk +0.05%. Six more 10 MB slices, reversed against forward: at 10 MB into the chain +0.11%, 20 MB −0.54%, 35 MB +0.04%, 50 MB −0.17%, 70 MB −0.12%, 85 MB +0.07% (mean of seven −0.11%, a lot of scatter). Full enwik8, champion settings, level 9, decode verified: 18,407,280 B against 18,393,007 forward, +14,273 B (+0.078%); total 18,430,989. The slices were noise; the forward chain stays.

                • Page headers: fields the decoder already knows

                  champion

                  From "should the revision headers be a constant?" Per-byte surprise on the 10 MB slice: headers are 2.8% of the bytes and 1.2% of the bits (241 bits a page). The fixed markup is nearly free already (33 bits a page); the cost is in the fields: comment 61, ids 53, title 35, username 26, timestamp 24. Some fields are stored in a relation the decoder already has, so they can be cut from the text and rebuilt after decoding: position as storage. Page ids rise with original position on all 12,347 pages (rebuilt from the permutation plus the gaps); revision ids follow their timestamps (MediaWiki numbers edits in time order; predicted from earlier pages, median error 23); a contributor id always goes with its username (cut when the username was seen before; 55% of named contributors).

                  Page ids, full enwik8, level 9: 18,364,888 B + map 4,959 + permutation 18,750 + side stream 1,794 = 18,390,391 B, −26,325 B (−0.14%) against 18,416,716; decoded and rebuilt to enwik8 byte for byte (pageid.py, run_full8.py TRANSFORM=noid). All three fields (headers.py, rebuild verified on enwik8): 652,966 B of text cut for a 16,736 B side stream; on the 10 MB slice's 824 whole pages, level 6: 2,071,896 → 2,066,319 B, about −0.21% after the side stream, against −0.09% for page ids alone. All three, full enwik8, level 9: 18,322,317 B + map + permutation + side stream 16,736 = 18,362,762 B, −53,954 B (−0.29%) against 18,416,716; decoded and rebuilt to enwik8 byte for byte (run_full8.py TRANSFORM=hdr). Titles as recently seen links: only 9–17% hit and lpaq already codes those cheaply; ~0.5 KB, dropped. Edit comments moved after the article text (commentmove.py, no side stream, round trip verified on enwik8): +0.007% with lpaq, +0.005% with xz on the 10 MB pages; the comment is predicted as well from its place by the contributor as from the text. Dropped. Model-independent on 10 MB (all three fields, side stream counted): lpaq host −0.27%, plain lpaq1 −0.34% (2,188,490 → 2,180,980, round trip verified), xz −0.32%, so it should carry to cmix.

                • Round 10: immune memory keyed on map cells

                  candidate

                  The immune memory remembers "context → next byte" after a surprise; hits make the memory stronger and misses weaken it. Two changes. First, it now records after any surprise above 1 bit, where before it waited for a much bigger one: more memories are formed. Second, a second and third memory are keyed on where the text is on the map (the last two, three or four cells) instead of on the bytes. A place on the map remembers what came next there before.

                  10 MB slice, level 9, every run decoded and compared: byte memory with the lower threshold −0.19%; + memory on three cells −0.315%; on four cells −0.346%; memories on three cells and two cells together −0.375% (they stack); four cells and two cells −0.416%, four and three −0.411%. Round 11, co-stimulation (one mixer input from the memories voting together, as a T cell needs two signals): −0.039%, with the previous byte −0.046% on top of three + two cells. Keys on five, six or seven cells lose (+0.07% to +0.16%): four cells is the right length. Round 13, the stack (four + two cells, co-stimulation with the previous byte): 2,048,644 → 2,039,189 B, −0.462% against the host. Round 14, with the earlier survivors on top: + next-cell indirect −0.116%, + both next cell and the tree weight set −0.138% (labels counted): 2,034,834 B + map and tree 5,854 B, −0.598% against the host (map counted). They still add up. A candidate for a full enwik8 run. three cells plus fine regions −0.325% (coarser places add little). A bigger cap on strength does nothing. The cell memories are the map-native part, about −0.15% on top of the byte memory. No side data: the memories are rebuilt while decoding.

                • Rounds 23–25: mixer weights chosen by where the text is

                • Rounds 38–42: what the article is about, and when the article changes

                  champion

                  The ChatGPT review's ideas (time, causes, entities, novelty, geography), each against a control, kept only if the whole file gets smaller. As article orders: years mentioned, edit time, link targets, the link graph and geography. In the model: word novelty (has this word ever been seen?), the article's title and its last link as contexts. Then a causal analysis of the one that worked, and three fixes aimed at what it found.

                  Orders (10 MB slices, level 6, all decoded, against the word chain): years +0.2% to +1.4%, edit time +1.2% to +2.2%, link targets −0.2% to +0.95%, link-graph bonus +0.27% to +0.76% (its random-pair control +1.2% to +2.0%: links are real but lose to words), geography too sparse (11–25 pages per slice with coordinates). A route solver (2-opt + Or-opt) won −0.07% to −0.14% on all three slices but lost on the full file twice (+23,941 B, +29,056 B): extinct. In the model (round 38): the title as a context −0.41% on "ten", but a page counter in its place −0.44%: the gain is scope (which article am I in), not the title's meaning; novelty −0.06% against a control at +0.04%; last link, difficulty-keyed weights lose. Held out at level 9: article scope + novelty −0.36% and −0.70%. Causal analysis (causal_page.py): ids that change every N bytes regardless of articles lose the whole gain; an id 1 KB late keeps a third to a half of it; the id × current word context carries it all. The saving is on words repeated within the article (+73k bits on 10 MB), concentrated on title words (3.6% of bytes, 35% of the saving), and paid back on words new to the article (−45k bits). Round 40: a mixer weight set by "has this word prefix occurred in this article?" −0.03% to −0.06% (control +0.01%). Round 41: the subject name (the next letter of the title word being spelled) −0.06% to −0.14% (control −0.02% to −0.03%); the previous article's words as context lose (+0.04% to +0.10%, also on word-chain slices). Round 42, level 9: subject + article-hit set on full7 −0.12% to −0.26%, a full-run candidate. Full enwik8, full7 (full5 + article scope + novelty) on the word chain: 17,606,758 B + 6,494 + 18,750 + 16,736 = 17,648,738 B, −34,412 B (−0.195%) against 17,683,150; decoded and rebuilt to enwik8 byte for byte. The full file gains about a third to a half of what the 10 MB slices showed. Full enwik8, full8 (full7 + article-hit weight set + subject name): 17,581,133 B + 41,980 = 17,623,113 B, −25,625 B (−0.145%) against 17,648,738; decoded and rebuilt byte for byte. Round 43: a count of earlier uses in the article (as weight set or context) and bolded lead terms as subject aliases: neutral or worse against controls, dropped. Round 44: section scope (article × heading count × word) neutral, dropped. Learning curves (per-byte costs on the full file): the stack's saving over full5 is flat along the file, 0.25% to 0.34% per 10 MB window (0.34% overall), so it is persistent; slices started fresh showed about twice that. The route order was 137 KB behind after its first tenth and won every later tenth, ending +24 KB: the loss is where articles sit in the stream, not their new neighbours. Vocabulary activation probe (offline): word associations from the article's prefix predict its coming known words 0.17 to 0.32 bits better than document frequency (13.6 bits), a random prefix 0.00: real but about 2%, below the 0.5-bit bar for a build. The route solver without reversals on the first 30 MB of articles: +0.009% (with reversals +0.50%): reversals were the cause, and without them the order is neutral; the word chain stays. Transfer test (full enwik8, all archives decompressed): word chain + header transform against the original order: zlib −4.43%, bzip2 −4.73%, zstd −1.76%, xz −1.44%, plain lpaq1 −2.16%, permutation and side stream counted: the order and header ideas do not depend on the host.                 • Round 9: the fluid map

                  no gain

                  The particles allowed to keep moving while the text streams, so the decoder replays the same physics and nothing is sent. Every cell is a particle on a torus; its mass is how often it has been seen. Each byte, the cell that just ended and the one before it pull together, each moving in proportion to the other's mass (inertia), and the newest cell is pushed out of crowded spots (pressure). Contexts from each cell's current place; also a mixer weight set by place (physics on the mixer instead of the regions). Start from a "big bang" of hashed positions (free) or the map plane.

                  10 MB, level 6, all decode-verified: moving fluid +0.16% (slow or fast attraction alike, +0.16%/+0.17%); no pressure +0.004%; frozen at the start −0.01%; starting from the map plane +0.21% (with its 1,280 B); weight set by place +0.03% (frozen −0.02%). Motion relabels the cells, and the statistics learned under the old places are lost; settled (frozen) it adds nothing beyond the static regions. The physics has to finish before the model starts learning, which is what the static map already is.

                • Article order by physics

                  lost

                  Physics on the article order instead of the map. One 10 MB slice's articles as particles on a line, starting at their chain position, pulled toward their 10 most similar articles, the line kept incompressible (evenly spaced) after every step; also with long articles heavier, and the springs' resting state solved outright (Fiedler vector).

                  Slice at 10 MB into the chain: physics +0.97%, heavier long articles +1.21%, resting state +0.86%; at 20 MB: +0.52%, +0.28%, +0.80% (decode-verified; stopped after two slices). Similarity between neighbouring articles falls from 0.55–0.62 to 0.12–0.25: the springs place each article near its group but not next to its closest relative, and compression pays for the next article being similar. The greedy chain stays.

                • The text as a Markov chain on the map

                  already covered

                  Held-out bits per token predicting the next cell or region from the k before it (counts from the first 80% of 5 MB, scored on the last 20%), the 16-region transition matrix, how fast it forgets, and whether it looks the same run backwards.

                  Cells: order 0 9.20, 1 6.87, 2 6.25, 3 6.24 bits a token (memory runs out after two cells). Regions: best at order 4, 2.57 bits (order 1 2.72). Second eigenvalue 0.71: the chain forgets which region it was in after ~3.5 steps. Strongly one-way: entropy production 1.52 of the 2.69 bits a step, mostly because the tokenizer's merges run left to right. lpaq1g's byte, word and region contexts already reach further than order 2 over cells, so no new context comes out of it.

                • Round 8: regions from a particle layout

                  no gain

                  From the Maps' use counts as density: each cell a particle with mass √uses, pulled together along the transition graph, pushed apart by mass, heavy cells sinking to the middle (ForceAtlas2-style, 16 dimensions, 300 steps), then the same k-means as the champion map. Control: every cell mass 1. Same map cost as the champion.

                  25 runs, all decode-verified. Against the champion map, level 6: mass √uses +0.20% on average (+0.14% on 10 MB), mass 1 +0.24% (+0.18%). The pull of use counts does help (it balances the regions: largest holds 12% of the text, against 28% without mass and 26% in the champion) but the layout throws away more than it adds; the spectral (PCA) embedding keeps more of what follows each cell. A springs-only layout lost the same way before.
